Open decisions — each one blocks something specific

Two price sets exist and they disagree

The Phase 1 spec puts done-for-you at $5,000–$15,000. The offer architecture on file puts the same stage at $2,500–$25,000, and its ascension path prices the strategy call at $297–$997 and the digital products at $27–$297 — none of which match the spec's $11–$111 DIY band.

The shipped pages carry the Phase 1 spec numbers. The offer architecture numbers are labelled estimates in their own document, so the spec won. That is a choice, and it should be a deliberate one.

Needs: Luckie to confirm one set

Row 1 and row 6 availability is unresolved

The compliance work on file finds that credit health and funding qualification carry criminal exposure in Georgia, and it marks its own conclusion as needing counsel before a contract is signed. The pricing page therefore states the unbundling and the licensed-professional model, and makes no state-specific claim in either direction.

Nothing on these pages sells credit repair, prices a credit outcome or promises a score change. That is deliberate and it should stay that way until a lawyer says otherwise.

Needs: counsel sign-off

The Hero gold is a proposal, not an approval

The brief says "black and gold, lightning logo" and names no gold. Three values and a placeholder bolt are on the brand page to keep the sub-brand usable meanwhile. One token change propagates to every Hero surface once the real one is named.

Needs: Luckie's gold + final vector

Strategy call price is not stated anywhere

The spec does not price the call and the public site does not either. The page commits to the deliverable — the written roadmap, kept whether or not you buy — and defers price to the calendar. If the call is meant to be paid, the calendar has to say so.

Needs: a number, or a decision that it is free
