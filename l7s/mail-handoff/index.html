Where l7sinc.com mail is actually going

The domain transfer to Spaceship replaced the Google Workspace mail records. Inbound mail is being delivered somewhere nobody is reading it.

For Luckie Goggins Domain l7sinc.com Measured Sep 3, 2026 · 9:32 PM EDT Source launch2.spaceship.net (authoritative)
Current state

All mail to admin@, hero@ and luckieg@ is routing to Spacemail, not to Google.

This is a verified routing fact, checked against the authoritative nameserver and confirmed by an independent public resolver. What is not yet verified is the outcome — whether those messages are sitting in a Spacemail inbox or bouncing. That check is outstanding and is listed below rather than guessed at.

01 What happened

l7sinc.com was transferred to Spaceship as registrar. Spaceship applies its own Spacemail preset to a domain on transfer, and that preset writes its own mail records into the zone — displacing the Google Workspace records that had been routing the three mailboxes.

Nobody chose this. It is the registrar's default behaviour on transfer, and it happens silently.

04 The blocker

The two Spacemail records cannot be deleted from the Advanced DNS page. They sit in a preset-owned record group with no delete control — unlike the Google record, which sits in the editable default group.

The route through is the domain's mail-service selector, not the record list. Releasing the preset returns the zone to manual control.

One trap worth naming. Adding a mail record while a conflicting one exists raises an “Add conflicting MX record?” dialog. The record is not saved until its confirm button is pressed — so a save that appears to do nothing is usually this dialog waiting off-screen.

05 The decision

Two coherent end states. Both are legitimate; they differ in cost and in how much work sits behind them.

Option A

Restore Google Workspace

Mail returns to the inboxes already in use. Nothing changes for anyone's daily habits, and every existing Google login keeps working untouched.

~$40 / month
Option B · lower cost

Commit to Spacemail

Spacemail is already the live route, so mail is arriving there now. The work is in the logins that hang off the Workspace accounts, not in the mail itself.

<$3 / month · first month free

The login question

This is the part worth being precise about. A Google Workspace account is also a Google login. Anything signed in with an @l7sinc.com address — Drive files, a YouTube channel, an Ads account, any third-party service using “Sign in with Google” — is attached to that Google account, not to the mailbox. Moving mail does not move those.

The recovery path is straightforward where a service does not accept the Google login afterwards: a password reset to the mailbox restores access, because the reset email goes to the address, and the address still works. What it does not recover is anything living inside Google — Drive contents, channel ownership — which has to be transferred deliberately before the Workspace account is closed.

Recommended sequence

  1. Restore Google first, so mail is unambiguously flowing again.
  2. Inventory what actually signs in with an @l7sinc.com Google account.
  3. Move or re-key those, one at a time, with mail working throughout.
  4. Migrate to Spacemail on a schedule, and close Workspace last.

Doing both at once means a mail outage and a login audit happening in the same window, which is how something quietly gets lost.

Why this took a day

Two things are worth stating rather than letting pass quietly.

Mail was reported as working when it was not. That reading came from a cached DNS lookup. The authoritative server said something different the whole time. A cached answer is not the truth of a zone that has just changed hands.

The DNS settings were not captured before the transfer. A pre-transfer snapshot would have made restoring the four lost records a two-minute job instead of a reconstruction. A snapshot has been taken now and is held with the client file.
