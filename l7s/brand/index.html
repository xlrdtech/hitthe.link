Phase 1 · The Visuals

One brand kit, two marks.

L7S Original is red, white and black. L7S Hero is the black-and-gold sub-brand with the lightning mark. Everything shipped in Phase 1 draws from the tokens on this page, so the funnel, the deck and the checkout read as one company instead of three vendors.

L7S Original — the primary palette

Red is the only accent. Black and white carry the structure; red carries attention, and nothing else competes for it. A second bright hue anywhere in the Original brand is a defect, not a variation.

Signal Red#D21F28The one accent. CTAs, marks, rules, active states.
Red Deep#A4151DHover and pressed only. Never a fill on its own.
Ink#0D0D0FBody text on light. Caption bars. Table headers.
Paper#FFFFFFPage ground in light. Text on ink.
Panel#F7F7F8Card and surface fill, one step off the ground.
Red Wash#FDF0F0Artifact bands and callouts. Never behind body copy.
Muted#6B6B73Secondary copy, deck text, table cells.
Line#E2E2E5Borders and dividers. 1px, never heavier.

The dark counterparts

Every page ships light and dark. Only the token values change — never the structure, never the hierarchy. Red lifts to #F04A52 on dark because #D21F28 loses contrast against near-black.

Signal Red · dark#F04A52The accent, lifted for contrast on ink grounds.
Paper · dark#0D0D0FPage ground. Always explicit on body — never transparent.
Panel · dark#161619Card fill, one step up from the ground.

Black and gold, lightning mark. Hero is the superhero-operating-system face of the company — The League, the S.O.S.™ deployment, the seven heroes. It never appears alongside Signal Red in the same composition; a surface is Original or it is Hero, never both.

Sign-off still owed on the gold

The brief names the Hero sub-brand as "black and gold, lightning logo" — it does not specify a gold. The three values above are a proposal built to hold contrast on #0A0A0B in both themes. They are not approved. Once Luckie names the gold, one token changes and every Hero surface follows.

Social proof — templates, not claims

L7S has one real customer quote on file and no published case studies. So this section ships the frames, empty, with the exact fields each one needs. Nothing here is filled with an invented name, an invented number or an invented result — a fabricated testimonial on a real company's site is the one failure that cannot be walked back.

"Thank you for the coordination and the education and matching me with someone so I didn't have to do it on my own, not knowing what to do or where to go... you saved me money and time and headache."

The one real quote on file. Three nouns in it — coordination, education, matching — and they are the whole business. Attribution and written permission are required before this appears on any public surface.
Pull quote · 1–3 sentences
The client's own words. Never rewritten, never tightened — the verbatim is the credibility.
Name + role + company
Full attribution, or the testimonial does not run.
System + tier
Which of the seven rows and which column, so the reader can locate themselves.
Written permission on file
Date and method. Required field.

What was actually broken, in plain language, with the starting figures.
What L7S coordinated
The cells engaged and the professionals matched. Coordination, education, matching — not "repair".

Measured, dated, and sourced to a document. No range, no "up to", no projection.

Why these pages are hand-built HTML and not a page builder

One self-contained file per page, all CSS inline, no framework and no build step. They load on a phone on a bad connection, they cost nothing to host, and there is no plugin that can expire and take the funnel down the week before launch.

The brand kit is the CSS custom properties at the top of each file — the same eight tokens, copied verbatim. Change a token, and the page changes with it.
