02Break-even — the arithmetic, in the open

Stripe is the live payment rail on the l7sinc.com page today, and it takes 2.9% + $0.30 per charge. Gross price is not what lands. Every quota below is computed on net.

// what actually lands from a sale of price p net(p) = p × (1 − 0.029) − $0.30 // how many of that unit clear one month of overhead units = overhead ÷ net(p), rounded UP to a whole sale // the overhead figure being cleared overhead = $459.56 / mo (full stack today) = $399.44 / mo (after the firm cuts land) // worked example, self-serve at $333 net(333) = 333 × 0.971 − 0.30 = $323.04 units = 459.56 ÷ 323.04 = 1.42 → 2 sales / month
The list gives the conversion bar a denominator

Luckie confirmed a reactivation campaign against 3,000 existing leads, targeting Q4 (September–October 2026). That turns every quota into a required conversion rate:

2 sales at $333 from 3,000 leads is 0.067%. One DFY flagship at $7,000 is 0.033%. 5 subscribers at $111/mo is 0.167%. The overhead bar is low against a list that size — the risk in this business is not break-even, it is delivery cost, which nothing on this page can measure.

04Recommended price — the paid self-serve checkout (Funnel B)

Derived from four constraints, not asserted. Each step is checkable.

Funnel B — self-serve checkout
$333
one-time  ·  or $111/mo recurring
  • Constraint 1 — the ceiling. LEVEL 1 DIY self-serve is defined as under $777. So p < 777.
  • Constraint 2 — the lattice. qi's verified unit price is $111 (invoice DCZ-001: $111 intro site + $111 social setup, $111 deposit received 2026-06-17), and the live DCZ retainer at hitthe.link/dcz/retainer is 4 × $111 = $444. His pricing language is already built in $111 multiples — and the $777 ceiling is itself 7 × $111. So the candidate set is $111n for n = 1…6.
  • Constraint 3 — clear overhead on low volume. At $333, 2 sales/month clear the full $459.56 stack (1.42 → 2). After the firm cuts land it is 1.24 → still 2, so the answer is stable whether or not the mail migration happens — that robustness is why $333 wins over $222, which needs 3 in both cases.
  • Constraint 4 — do not crowd the tiers on either side. $333 sits 5.1× below the BAG fast-pass floor of $1,700, so it cannot cannibalize the fast-pass. It sits 1.8× above the UCESPP $188 entry, so it does not undercut the partner product. $222 is only $34 above UCESPP — too close to a thing L7S also sells.

Rows 5 and 6 turn on conversion, and there is no conversion data in any artifact. That part of the reasoning is judgment and is labelled as such. Everything in rows 1–4 is arithmetic.

The recurring alternative is stronger, if the offer can carry it

$111/mo needs 5 active subscribers to clear the full stack, or 4 after the firm cuts. That is a higher bar in month one and a far lower one in month six, because subscribers accumulate and one-time sales reset to zero every month. It also reuses the $111 unit exactly.

Same lattice, same anchor, different risk profile. $333 one-time is the recommendation for launch; $111/mo is the recommendation for the second iteration once there is retention data.
