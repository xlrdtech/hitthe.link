The caveat

"No personal guarantee" is carrying a lot of weight in that sentence

Collateral-coverage lending is real and the logic holds directionally. But a waived personal guarantee on an eight-figure acquisition is uncommon for a buyer with no operating track record in that industry. SBA 7(a) requires a guarantee from any owner of 20% or more, and most middle-market and commercial lenders ask as a matter of course.

He is borrowing with a decade of contracting operations and hospital-scale credentials behind him. The structure works partly because of who is asking. Read the ratio as necessary, not sufficient.

What transfers

Borrow against an asset that covers itself, rather than against yourself.

It is a different lane from services income. Services scale with hours and restart at zero every month. An acquisition financed against its own dissolution value is bought once and carries its own collateral.
